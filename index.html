About this parish

The parish was established in 1619 as a visita under the care of the Franciscan missionaries, serving the scattered settlements along the Libmanan River. It was raised to a full parish later that century, with St. James the Greater named as its patron. The present stone church was begun in the 1750s and completed in stages over the decades that followed. Its bell tower, added in the nineteenth century, still carries three of the original bells. The parish has served as the mother church of several communities that were themselves later erected into parishes of their own, and today remains the seat of the vicariate.

The problems worth knowing about

The decision the panel will ask about

Read live, not snapshotted

Every other value on a certificate comes from the jsonb snapshot taken at issuance — that is what makes it verifiable. The logo deliberately does not. It is letterhead, not certified content, so it is read at render time: one upload fixes every certificate including those already issued, and a sysadmin who uploads a bad file can repair it instead of reissuing. The signature is the opposite case and stays snapshotted.

Deliberate · opposite of the signature

A first for this codebase

The project's first writable global table

diocese is one row, forced: id smallint primary key default 1 with check (id = 1), seeded by the migration, following health_check. Every write is update … where id = 1, so "which logo?" has exactly one answer. RLS on, a for select to patron_app read policy and for all to patron_admin for writes — a global table with RLS and no policy denies everything, which is the default the project leans on.

New in kind · needs the read policy

The trap

The admin router has never taken a binary body

It needs its own express.raw({ type: LOGO_TYPES, limit: '1mb' }). The size ceiling is in SQL as well — check (octet_length(logo) <= 1048576) — so a bug in Express cannot put a 40 MB image into a backed-up table. Same belt-and-braces as parish.photo.

Mind the parser

The good news

Zero new error codes, and one function to change

MISSING_FILE, INVALID_FILE_TYPE, FILE_TOO_LARGE and IMAGE_NOT_FOUND already mean exactly this, so error_codes.md and lib/errors.js are untouched. And all three PDF routes — Path 1 staff, Path 1 parishioner, Path 2 event certificates — funnel through renderCertificatePdf, so threading one logo parameter covers every certificate the system can issue.

One migration · one function · no new codes
Why this is the honest counterpart to the seal. The diocese logo is a real mark, supplied by the people who own it, stored as the bytes they uploaded. That is precisely what the generated parish seal was not — and it is why the logo belongs on a certificate while the seal did not belong on a parish page.

Design notes

Pictures without a schema change

The page reads as text because six facts sit in one column and the history is one paragraph block. Every idea below fixes that using only the four columns you are already adding — none of them stores an image.

Banknotes · share certificates · laid paper

Backgrounds for the rail, not for everything

The right column read as four plain white boxes. Each now has a background that says something about the card: laid-paper rules on the plate, a nimbus on the feast, a monstrance burst behind the 406 years, and guilloche — the lathe-turned line work engravers put on banknotes precisely because it is hard to copy — on the card that issues certificates. Three are CSS gradients, one is a 64×26 SVG tile.

The restraint that matters here is what was left alone. The Parish office and Schedule cards on the left are still flat, on purpose: they are four label-and-value pairs and a block of times, and dense factual reading wants a quiet field behind it. Texture belongs on the ornamental rail, not under the Mass times a parishioner is trying to read.

Free · CSS and one SVG tile

Feather · Lucide · Phosphor — studied, not installed

A stroke icon set, drawn in the file

Twenty-two symbols on a 24×24 grid, stroke only, 1.6 units, round caps, no fills — the same discipline those sets use, which is why they sit quietly beside Cormorant rather than competing with it. They are drawn here, not imported: package.json is untouched, there is no icon font and no request, and the whole sprite is about 2.6 KB of markup.

Three of them earn their place rather than decorate. The Latin cross marks the Mass schedule, which is the one row on the card a parishioner actually came for. The same cross replaces the ✠ character in the fleuron and the titular plate — a text glyph that Cormorant Garamond does not contain, so today it silently falls back to another font and on some machines shows a blank box. And the saint glyph gives patron_saint a mark of its own in the directory, where it is the only line distinguishing one card from the next.

The restraint is deliberate: icons sit on labels, never on values. A phone glyph beside Contact number is a signpost; one beside the number itself would be noise.

Free · no dependency, no schema

Illuminated manuscripts · breviaries · missals

The drop cap as ornament

The oldest answer to a wall of text, and the right one for a parish. The first letter of the history is set in Cormorant at 3.4rem in a gold-ruled box. One CSS rule, applied to a column you already have.

Free · CSS only

Cut — and why

A generated parish seal

The first draft of this rail held a round seal: the patron curved on an arc, a cross, the year in Roman numerals. It looked the best of anything here and it was wrong.

A seal is an official mark, particular to one parish, pressed onto documents to authenticate them. A roundel generated from a text field is not that parish's seal — and in a system whose thesis claim is verifiable document issuance, a device that reads as authentication but authenticates nothing is the one ornament that cannot be afforded. The panel asks "is that the parish's real seal?" and the answer is no.

Cut · misrepresents an official mark

Letterpress nameplates · title pages · engraved plaques

A titular plate instead

Same two columns, none of the claim. Rectangular on purpose: a circle with curved lettering reads as a seal, a ruled plate reads as a title. No cross-and-circle device, no Roman numerals, no municipality ring — just the patron set large in Cormorant between two gold rules, and the founding year in small caps.

It also fixes the seal's practical defect. A 43-character title would not fit on a 230-unit arc; set as two lines of straight type it simply wraps.

Free · markup and CSS, no new column

Gothic tracery · rose windows · tile floors

A quatrefoil texture behind the prose

Four overlapping circles in a 44px tile, wine at 10% opacity, as an SVG data URI. It gives a text card a surface without an image request, an asset or a route. Use it on one card only — on every card it becomes noise.

Free · data URI, no asset

Ecclesiastical typography · service booklets

A fleuron rule instead of a border

A cross centred between two hairlines that fade out. It separates the eyebrow from the prose far better than a plain border-top. It began as the single character ✠ and is now the drawn #i-cross symbol, because Cormorant Garamond has no glyph for that code point.

Free · two strokes

Wall calendars · event listings

The feast day as a calendar leaf

The one with a catch. It needs the day and the month apart, and feast_day is deliberately free text so a parish can write "Last Sunday of January" or "Moveable — Pentecost". Parsing it would work for most and look broken for the rest. Show the leaf only when the text matches a day-and-month pattern, and fall back to the plain line otherwise.

Needs care · free text will not always parse

Museum labels · National Trust property pages

The age, derived

406 years from year_established and this year, set in PATRON's existing .stat. A derived number carries more than the raw one and costs a subtraction.

Free · derived, nothing stored

What I would not do

A photo gallery, a timeline, a Mass-times grid

All three need schema. A gallery needs a second photo table — parish_photo_public holds exactly one. A timeline needs dated events, not one founding year. A day-by-day Mass grid needs structured schedule rows, which is already a deferral in backlog.md. None is worth opening after the defense.

Needs schema · leave it
If you take three, take these. The drop cap, the year watermark and the titular plate. They are the whole difference between a text page and a designed one, they touch no migration, and all three come out of the two columns — patron_saint and year_established — that the panel asked for in the first place.

And if a parish wants its real seal shown, that is an upload, not a drawing: another bytea column, a public route with Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy, and a 404 path for parishes without one. It is the identical pattern to the diocese logo in phase 3, so it belongs there if it belongs anywhere — not invented here.
